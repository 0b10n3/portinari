A flat papercut elevation, seen straight on: a flat octagonal paper token floats motionless in an empty sky at the very top of its toss, and in the floor directly beneath it, sharing its vertical centre line, a row of three octagonal holes, two equal small ones and one large one, marks the exact place where it is about to land. An open paper hand in the lower left corner has just tossed it.

Only two sheets of the stack appear in this picture. The flat sky sheet is level 1 of the stack, the darkest sheet, and reaches all four edges of the picture, behind everything. Over its lower part lies the flat floor band, cut from level 4 of the stack, the lightest sheet of the dark stack, exactly that tone: a single sheet running from the left edge to the right edge and covering a little more than the lower third of the picture's height, its top edge cut perfectly straight and horizontal. The floor is seen straight on, exactly like the sky, as an upright stripe. The sky fills the upper two thirds of the picture, calm and open.

The token is off-centre, to the right. The token and the large opening share one vertical centre line, three fifths of the way across the picture from the left edge, and both sit entirely in the right half of the picture, one exactly above the other: the token high in the sky, its centre about a quarter of the way down from the top edge, and the large opening low in the floor, directly beneath the token, centred in the floor band's height. Between them there is only sky and the straight top edge of the floor. The picture's own middle line passes through the second small opening, and the sky above the two small openings stays empty.

The token is about one seventh of the picture's width. It is a regular octagon with a flat top: horizontal top and bottom sides, vertical left and right sides, and four diagonal sides at 45 degrees. It faces the viewer squarely, upright and untilted, flat as an architectural elevation, so only its front face shows, and it touches nothing. Its rim is a ring of eight trapezoid pieces, cut one by one and butted together along thin straight seams that run from each inner corner to the matching outer corner. All eight are cut from the paler figure colour, the second of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone: cool, soft and pale, the same flat tone in every piece, held well apart from the accent. Inside the ring sits a smaller concentric octagon, about three fifths of the token's width, with the same flat-top orientation, cut from the deeper figure colour, the first of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone, and laid on top like a second flat sheet. Both octagons are plain, unprinted paper.

One trapezoid of the ring, the one on the upper-right diagonal side, between the top side and the right side, is cut from the accent tone: a single accent trapezoid, its outer edge meeting the empty sky. It is the only accent in the whole picture; the other seven trapezoids, the inner octagon and every other shape keep their own tones. This trapezoid, at the top of the toss, is the focal point.

The floor holds one row of three openings on a single horizontal centre line in the middle of the floor band's height, with plain floor above and below them. Read the row from left to right, measured in widths of one small opening: a small opening one unit wide, a bridge half a unit wide, a second small opening one unit wide, a second bridge half a unit wide, then the large opening three units wide. The row has only two sizes. The two small octagonal cut-outs are a matched pair, identical twins with the same width, the same height and the same cut, each exactly one third of the large opening's width. The large octagonal cut-out closes the row at its right end, directly beneath the token, sharing its vertical centre line; it is nearly the token's own size, nine tenths of its width. All three keep the token's flat-top orientation. The two narrow bridges of floor paper between them are equal and straight, so the three octagons stay separate and whole. To the right of the large opening the floor runs plain and empty all the way to the right edge. Each opening is a clean hole through the floor sheet: inside it lies only the flat sky sheet behind, the same level 1 tone as the sky above, meeting the floor at a crisp cut line. Together the three empty openings add up to the token.

In the lower left corner, a faceted paper hand enters from the left edge of the picture, its wrist cut off by that edge, and lies over the floor band. It is open and palm up, held flat like a hand that has just tossed something upward and stays open: the four fingers straight and pressed together, the thumb lying flat along the side of the index finger, so the whole hand is one single long, low polygon tapering to a blunt, straight-cut tip that points right, toward the token. The hand rises gently from the wrist, and its fingertips lift just above the top edge of the floor band, about a quarter of the way across the picture, far to the left of the small openings. It is cut from one smooth, unbroken sheet of the deeper figure colour, the first of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone, the same flat tone from wrist to fingertips; its facets are only the short straight cuts along its outline, and inside the outline the sheet is one plain surface.

Between the fingertips and the token there is only open sky, and to the right of the token and of the large opening, sky and floor are empty and calm. The sky holds only the token and the fingertips. Everything important sits well inside the picture: the token stays comfortably below the top edge, the openings stay comfortably above the bottom edge, and the token and the large opening stay well away from the right edge. Only the sky sheet and the floor band carry a faint paper fibre grain; the token, its accent trapezoid and the hand are clean, smooth paper.
