A flat papercut elevation, seen straight on: a flat octagonal paper token floats motionless in an empty sky at the very top of its toss, and in the floor directly beneath it, sharing its vertical centre line, a row of three octagonal holes, two equal small ones and one large one, marks the exact place where it is about to land. A small open paper hand in the lower left corner has just tossed it.

Only two sheets of the stack appear in this picture. The flat sky sheet is level 1 of the stack, the darkest sheet, and reaches all four edges of the picture, behind everything. Over its lower part lies the flat floor band, cut from level 4 of the stack, the lightest sheet of the dark stack, exactly that tone: a single sheet covering a little more than the lower third of the picture's height, its top edge cut perfectly straight and horizontal. Like the sky sheet, the floor sheet is trimmed flush with the picture's own edges: it runs all the way to the left edge, all the way to the right edge and all the way down to the bottom edge, so both bottom corners of the picture are floor, and the sky shows only above the floor's straight top edge and inside the three openings. The floor is seen straight on, exactly like the sky, as an upright stripe. The sky fills the upper two thirds of the picture, calm and open.

The whole picture is laid out with the token's own width as the ruler. Read it from left to right: the hand, reaching in from the left edge about one and a half token widths; a stretch of plain floor about one token wide; the row of three openings, a little under two token widths long; and then, to the right of the token and of the large opening, empty sky and plain floor about two token widths wide, running to the right edge. The token and the large opening share one vertical centre line, a little right of the picture's centre: the picture's own vertical middle line passes just to the left of both of them, through the second small opening, and the empty sky to the right of the token is at least as wide as the token itself, closer to twice its width. The token hangs high in the sky, its centre about a quarter of the way down from the top edge, with a stretch of empty sky between its bottom side and the floor's top edge about as tall as the token itself. The large opening sits directly beneath it, low in the floor, centred in the floor band's height. Between them there is only sky and the straight top edge of the floor. The sky above the two small openings stays empty.

The token and the large opening are a matched pair, nearly the same size. The large opening is the token's own outline cut just a sliver smaller, so the token is only a hair wider than the opening: dropped straight down, the token would cover the opening almost exactly, edge for edge. The large opening fills about two thirds of the floor band's height, with an equal strip of plain floor above and below it, and the token is that same size plus a sliver.

The token is a regular octagon with a flat top: horizontal top and bottom sides, vertical left and right sides, and four diagonal sides at 45 degrees. It faces the viewer squarely, upright and untilted, flat as an architectural elevation, so only its front face shows, and it touches nothing. Its rim is a ring of eight trapezoid pieces, cut one by one and butted together along thin straight seams that run from each inner corner to the matching outer corner. All eight are cut from the paler figure colour, the second of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone: cool, soft and pale, the same flat tone in every piece, held well apart from the accent. Inside the ring sits a smaller concentric octagon, about three fifths of the token's width, with the same flat-top orientation, cut from the deeper figure colour, the first of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone, and laid on top like a second flat sheet. Both octagons are plain, unprinted paper.

One trapezoid of the ring, the one on the upper-right diagonal side, between the top side and the right side, is cut from the accent tone: a single accent trapezoid, its outer edge meeting the empty sky. It is the only accent in the whole picture; the other seven trapezoids, the inner octagon and every other shape keep their own tones. This trapezoid, at the top of the toss, is the focal point.

The floor holds one row of three openings on a single horizontal centre line in the middle of the floor band's height, with plain floor above and below them. Read the row from left to right: a small opening, a narrow bridge, a second small opening of exactly the same size, a second narrow bridge of the same width, then the large opening. The row has only two sizes. The two small octagonal cut-outs are truly small, a matched pair, identical twins with the same width, the same height and the same cut: each one is only about twice as wide as the narrow bridge of floor beside it, which makes each one third of the large opening's width, and the two small openings together with the bridge between them span a little less than the large opening alone. The large octagonal cut-out closes the row at its right end, directly beneath the token, sharing its vertical centre line. All three keep the token's flat-top orientation. The two narrow bridges of floor paper between them are equal and straight, each about half as wide as a small opening, so the three octagons stay separate and whole. To the right of the large opening the floor runs plain and empty all the way to the right edge. Each opening is a clean hole through the floor sheet: inside it lies only the flat sky sheet behind, the same level 1 tone as the sky above, meeting the floor at a crisp cut line. Together the three empty openings add up to the token.

In the lower left corner, a small faceted paper hand enters from the left edge of the picture, its wrist cut off by that edge, and lies over the floor band. It is open and palm up, held flat like a hand that has just tossed something upward and stays open: the four fingers straight and pressed together, the thumb lying flat along the side of the index finger, so the whole hand is one single long, low polygon tapering to a blunt, straight-cut tip that points right, toward the token. It is a modest supporting shape: from the left edge to its fingertips it is shorter than the row of three openings, and its height is about half the token's height. The hand rises gently from the wrist, and its fingertips stop about a quarter of the way across the picture, lifting just above the top edge of the floor band. Between the fingertips and the first small opening lies a stretch of plain, empty floor about as wide as the token. The hand is cut from one smooth, unbroken sheet of the deeper figure colour, the first of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone, the same flat tone from wrist to fingertips; its facets are only the short straight cuts along its outline, and inside the outline the sheet is one plain surface.

Between the fingertips and the token there is only open sky, and to the right of the token and of the large opening, sky and floor are empty and calm. The sky holds only the token and the fingertips. The token and the three openings sit well inside the picture: the token comfortably below the top edge, the openings comfortably above the bottom edge, and the token and the large opening well away from the right edge. Only the sky sheet and the floor band carry a faint paper fibre grain; the token, its accent trapezoid and the hand are clean, smooth paper.
