A flat papercut elevation, seen straight on: the same coin twice, side by side at the centre of a calm, empty picture. There are exactly two tokens, two identical octagonal paper tokens with the same size, the same outline and the same face, sitting on one shared horizontal centre line. The one on the right is a single whole piece. The one on the left is the same token in three pieces, bound together by one single thin accent band.

The background is a single flat background sheet, level 1 of the stack, the darkest sheet: one plain, unbroken sheet trimmed flush with all four edges of the picture, the same tone from the top edge to the bottom edge and from the left edge to the right edge. It is the only thing behind the two tokens, and they rest on nothing and touch nothing. The whole picture holds just four things: this sheet, the two tokens and the one band.

The pair is small and compact, a modest cluster centred in a wide, calm field. Together the two tokens and the gap between them span only the middle half of the picture's width, so the empty background on each side of the pair is at least as wide as a whole token, and each token is well under half the picture's height. The empty background is by far the largest part of the picture. The picture's own vertical middle line runs down through the gap between the twins. The shared centre line of the two tokens sits just above the picture's horizontal middle, and above and below the pair the empty background is broad, so both tokens sit comfortably inside the picture, far from every edge. Neither token is the main one: they are twins of exactly equal size and equal weight, their top sides at one height and their bottom sides at one height.

Each token is a regular octagon with a flat top: horizontal top and bottom sides, vertical left and right sides, and four diagonal sides at 45 degrees. It faces the viewer squarely, upright and untilted, flat as an architectural elevation. Its outer octagon is cut from the paler figure colour, the second of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone. On its face lies a smaller concentric octagon, about three fifths of the token's width, with the same flat-top orientation, cut from the deeper figure colour, the first of the two figure colours in the palette, exactly that tone, and laid on top like a second flat sheet. Both tokens carry this same design and nothing more: plain, unprinted paper.

The token on the right is the whole octagonal paper token: one single piece, intact from edge to edge, its smaller octagon whole.

The token on the left is the same token in three pieces: exactly three pieces, separated by exactly two vertical gaps, two straight cuts that run through it from its top edge to its bottom edge, parallel to each other and identical in width, each about a tenth of the token's width. Both cuts lie in the token's slanted left end, the part between its vertical left side and the two points where its left diagonal sides meet the flat top and bottom sides. The second cut runs down right through those two points; the first cut runs midway between the second cut and the vertical left side. So, from left to right, the token is a narrow strip, a second narrow strip of exactly the same width, and one wide piece with all the rest: the whole flat-topped body and the right end, about two thirds of the token. The first strip is plain outer octagon; the second strip carries the left tip of the smaller octagon, which carries on across the second cut into the wide piece. Each cut is a clean opening through the paper: inside it you see only the flat background sheet, the same tone as the empty background around the pair, meeting the paper at a crisp straight cut line. All three are pieces kept in register: each one stays exactly in its own place within the octagon outline, upright and level, never shifted, raised or lowered. Each keeps its own slice of the outline: the two narrow strips are shorter than the token, their upper and lower ends cut along the slanted sides, and the top and bottom sides of the wide piece stay level with the top and bottom sides of the whole token beside it. The left token keeps the full octagonal outline of its twin, simply cut into three.

The clear gap between the twins is plain background, twice as wide as one of the two cuts and much narrower than a token, so the row reads as two separate tokens, and the two cuts read as cuts inside one of them.

The focal point is the single thin accent band, cut from the accent tone: one straight, continuous strip of paper laid flat across the left token, horizontal, at the height of its centre. It binds the three pieces into one token. It runs over all three pieces and straight across both cuts without a break, so inside each cut it lies over the background sheet. It is very thin, a slender strip about one fortieth of the token's height, thinner than either cut. It is short, barely longer than the token is wide: each end passes the left token's vertical side by only a sliver, about the band's own thickness, and is cut straight and square. Both ends stay close to the left token, well inside the empty background and far from the picture's edges, and the right end leaves almost the whole gap between the twins open. The band belongs to the left token alone: it is the only band and the only accent in the whole picture, and the right token, both smaller octagons and the background keep their own tones.

Only the background sheet carries a faint paper fibre grain; the two tokens and the band are clean, smooth paper. Around the pair there is only calm, empty background.
