A single large cut-paper object sits centred on an empty field: a coffee sack whose sewn mouth has been opened, so that the same cloth leaves the seam already turned into a plain sheet that folds and comes to rest on a low pile. One object, one idea, one material in two states. No person, no hand, no arm, no silhouette of a worker anywhere in the frame; no landscape, no drying yard, no warehouse, no horizon.

Composition reads left to right across a wide empty ground that fills well over forty percent of the picture. The left half holds a standing full coffee sack with pleated mouth, seen straight on, broad and heavy at the base, the mouth gathered into a few straight creases. Its body carries flat crossed jute weave bands — few, wide, cut at right angles, suggested as strips of paper, never as photographic fibre. Low on the flank sits a stitched patch and frayed corner on the burlap: the patch a plain rectangle sewn on, the bottom corner cut away tooth by tooth into short blunt teeth. The sack body is the primary figure tone; the weave bands, the patch and the frayed corner are the secondary figure tone, one step lighter, so the material reads without noise.

Across the mouth runs a row of hand-sewn bag mouth stitches in that same secondary figure tone — wide, uneven, clearly made by hand and not by a machine. The focal point is the exact place where that row stops, slightly right of centre and just above the middle of the frame: the open seam where jute weave becomes a smooth sheet. Left of that point the plane is woven; right of it the same plane is bare and uniform. One single pulled cotton stitching thread lifts out of the seam there, a thin loose line, and it is the only element in the accent tone — the accent appears in that one thread and nowhere else, not on cut edges, not on the folds, not on the pile, not on the base line.

From that point the plane continues right as a smooth sheet folding once at 45 degrees, flat and untextured, descending to a low stack of blank sheets with perforated edge and folded corner: sheets slipped slightly off one another, the top edge picked into small regular perforations, the front sheet's corner turned over. Sheet and stack are the fourth, lightest stack layer, clearly separated from the sack. The stack ends well inside the frame, stopping before the right seventy-eight hundredths of the width, with open ground beyond it; the focal point and everything essential stay far from all four edges, since the picture will be cropped towards the centre.

The foreground base line is three flat wooden pallet slats, straight and flat, in the second stack layer. Resting on them: a curved sacking needle with a short length of thread coiled around it, lying at the foot of the sack in the secondary figure tone; a short stevedore bag hook leaning against the lower flank, its point turned upward, in the second stack layer; and three loose coffee beans with center crease, oval, seen from the side, each with its diagonal crease, all three the same bean shape, spilled on the slats in the secondary figure tone. The beans stay beans — they do not change shape, do not flatten, do not become anything else. The one transformation in the picture happens at the seam, nowhere else. The background is the first stack layer, empty and uninterrupted above and to both sides.

Everything is cut paper: hard straight edges and 45 degree cuts, uniform flat tones, no volume, no shine, no texture inside a shape. The fold is a crisp 45 degree crease, never a soft curve of cloth.

Nothing in the image is readable. The sack is completely bare: no stencil, no origin mark, no weight mark, no crop-year mark, no brand, no stamp, no seal, no tag, no label. The sheets on the right are completely empty: no ruled lines, no certificate border, no ornamental frame, no rosette, no ribbon, no seal, no signature line, no watermark. No letters, no words, no initials, no numerals of any kind anywhere in the frame, not even small, decorative or illegible.
